The modelling work comprised two kinds of Design, Build, Test, Learn (DBTL) cycle. This page describes the strain-design cycle, in which the Test stage indicated that the design should be changed, followed by the structure-validation cycles (six Boltz-2 rounds and six independent-check cycles, including the failures), starting at How the cycles were run. The dry-lab cycles are model against model and model against literature; they feed the wet-lab cycles described in Shared cycles with the wet lab.

Design

In brief

The goal was to choose genetic edits that move yeast’s fatty acids toward myristic acid (C14:0). Two ideas were considered: a single mutation in the fatty acid synthase, and gene deletions that reduce fat breakdown, ranked with a metabolic model.

Goal. Selection of genetic edits that shift the fatty-acid profile of Saccharomyces cerevisiae toward C14:0.

Initial plan. Two strategies were considered: (1) a FAS2 point mutation to release C14 early (by analogy with Yarrowia lipolytica I1220W), and (2) gene knockouts that reduce fatty-acid degradation, in order to preserve C14:0. Candidate knockouts were to be ranked by agreement between the predicted fatty-acid profile and a target, using flux balance analysis (FBA) on Yeast9.

Build

In brief

Three tools were built: a fast metabolic calculator with a scoring function, three ways of searching gene-deletion combinations, and a pipeline that predicts how a fatty acid sits in the enzyme.

  • A fast FBA evaluator and a scoring function over fatty-acid profiles.
  • A search over knockout combinations (up to three at a time): brute force, greedy and a reinforcement-learning agent.
  • A structural pipeline for the FAS2 ketoacyl synthase (KS) domain: homology-based site identification and Boltz-2 ligand placement.

Test

In brief

The tests undermined the plan. The ranking had almost no margin over the unedited strain, the model showed myristic acid arising only from fat breakdown, a learning-based search lost to simpler ones, and early structure predictions depended on setup.

  • Brute-force evaluation of 175 combinations returned a leading set (FAA1 + FAA4, score 0.202529) against 0.202509 for the unedited strain, a margin of 0.010 %. Gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC-MS) composition varies by 1–5 % between replicates, so no experiment could separate the two. Pre-flight, a set of six automated checks, was therefore built, and the team’s own scoring function failed three of them.
  • Route analysis of the FBA solution showed that the model forms C14:0 only by shortening C16:0 through β-oxidation. Deletion of POX1 gave exactly zero.
  • Benchmarking of the reinforcement-learning agent over 20 seeds showed that it found the exact best answer in 10 % of runs, compared with 65 % for greedy search and 100 % for brute force.
  • The first structural runs suggested that unconstrained Boltz-2 does not place the ligand and that the acyl chain overlaps M1251. Reruns with five samples reproduced neither observation, so both are treated as dependent on the setup.

Learn

In brief

Five lessons followed: the deletion strategy was reversed, one design (the single mutation) went to the laboratory, the model needed a hand-added reaction, simple search was sufficient, and one residue was excluded as a likely gate.

  • The degradation-knockdown strategy was inverted. In the model, degradation is the only source of C14:0, and the strategy was therefore not pursued.
  • The scoring function could not support any ranking, so the wet lab received one design, the single mutation.
  • The metabolic model could not represent the intended intervention. Yeast9 has no early C14 release from FAS, and a documented reaction was therefore added.
  • Simple search was sufficient. At the present scale, exact enumeration of up to three simultaneous knockouts finishes in minutes to under two hours for up to 50 candidate genes.
  • M1251 was deprioritised as a mutation site as a precaution, on the literature suggestion that it is a conformational gate. Published S. cerevisiae strains with M1251W show that the position tolerates substitution and shifts chain length to C6/C8, so this is not a finding about the enzyme (see Step 2).

How the cycles were run

In brief

Each cycle asked one question, used the cheapest method that could answer it, and set its pass or fail rule before the result was known. A failed check ended or redirected that line of work.

  • Cheap checks first. Quick geometry checks and one positive control (Vina docking on the known complex 1F91, which failed) were run before the force-field simulations, and a simulation was run only on what survived the check before it. No positive control was run for Boltz-2 itself (see Outcome and limits).
  • Rules set beforehand. Thresholds and file hashes were recorded before each Boltz-2 batch was launched (record and criteria in Table 6 of the Step 2 page). The thresholds were fixed before the runs and were not changed after the data were seen.
  • Failures kept. Invalid analyses were renamed, not deleted, and the reason is given in the register below.
  • Same-study comparison only. Force-field energies and cavity volumes are used to compare runs within this study; they are not binding free energies.

Structure prediction: six Boltz-2 rounds (five complete, one mostly failed)

In brief

Six rounds of Boltz-2 runs: 25 runs and 125 models in rounds 2 to 5, plus a sixth round of seed replicates of which only two of eight runs finished. Each began from the previous result. The main outcome was a negative one: the variation between seeds was as large as the effect of the substitutions.

Round 1

Takeaway: The pose looked constraint-dependent.

  • Design. Does the ketoacyl synthase (KS) pocket hold a C14 chain, and which residues are off limits?
  • Build. C14 in the KS domain, unconstrained and with distance caps (6 and 8 Å); C16 and C22 as controls. Google Colab, one sample per run.
  • Test. Unconstrained ligand ipTM 0.511; constrained 0.80 to 0.83. An overlap with M1251 of 0.96 to 1.77 Å was reported.
  • Learn. The pose looked constraint-dependent, so the next round re-ran the setup on laboratory GPUs and tested the reported overlap.

Round 2

Takeaway: The reported M1251 overlap did not recur.

  • Design. Does the result depend on how the run is set up?
  • Build. 7 runs, 35 models on RTX A6000 GPUs: unconstrained baseline, cutoffs of 6, 8 and 10 Å, V1254W, C16 and C22; seed 42, soft contact on C1305.
  • Test. Baseline ligand 3.0 to 4.6 Å from C1305 (ipTM 0.80). M1251 no closer than 3.3 Å. M1260 at 15 to 17 Å.
  • Learn. The M1251 overlap did not recur. The pose seemed to follow the constrained residue, so the gate residues were constrained directly.

Round 3

Takeaway: Confidence did not separate the setups.

  • Design. Does the pose follow the residues it is told to touch?
  • Build. 8 runs, 40 models: forced contacts on M1251 and M1260 (one run also V1254), with steering potentials on or off.
  • Test. M1260 at 3.9 to 6.5 Å. No overlap with M1251 with or without potentials. Confidence 0.90 to 0.94 in every run.
  • Learn. Confidence did not separate the setups, so controls (a repeat, a pocket-plug mutant, a decoy ligand) were added.

Round 4

Takeaway: A same-seed repeat is not a replicate.

  • Design. Does the model respond to a change in the pocket, and does a rerun agree?
  • Build. 4 runs, 20 models in a 30-minute window: wild-type repeat, a four-tryptophan “plug” mutant, a caffeine decoy, V1254W.
  • Test. Same-seed repeat identical (0.00 Å). Plug moved the ligand 1.25 Å (ipTM 0.870 vs 0.861). V1254 distance 5.88 vs 6.33 Å.
  • Learn. A repeat with the same seed proves nothing about variation, so a second seed and more substitutions were run.

Round 5

Takeaway: Seed variation matched the substitution effects.

  • Design. How large is the variation between seeds, and what do further substitutions and ligands do?
  • Build. 6 runs, 30 models: wild type with seed 43, M1251W, V1254A, C22, C8 and a glucose decoy.
  • Test. Seed 43 vs 42: V1254 median +0.82 Å, ligand RMSD median 2.14 Å. C22 reached M1260 (6.9 Å vs 15.5 Å for C14).
  • Learn. Seed variation was as large as the substitution effects, so distances are read as descriptive and independent methods were added (cycles 0 to 5 below).

Round 6

Takeaway: Only 2 of 8 runs finished; the question is unresolved.

  • Design. Are the V1254W and plug effects larger than wild-type seed spread? Eight runs queued (wild type seeds 44 to 46, V1254W seeds 43 to 45, plug seeds 43 and 44), with the decision rule and five predictions fixed beforehand (17 October 2026).
  • Build. Same settings as round 5, three GPUs, a 30-minute window and a 16 GB free-memory gate.
  • Test. Plug seed 43 finished in 1,167 s; V1254W seed 43 wrote five models but was cut during the affinity step (1,802 s); wild-type seeds 44 and 45 were killed before writing a model; four runs never started. Server load was about 110 to 140 with all GPUs busy. The prediction that at least six of eight runs would finish failed (2 of 8). V1254 distance medians: wild type 6.33 and 7.15 Å, V1254W 5.88 and 4.61 Å, plug 6.79 and 7.41 Å.
  • Learn. Wild type still has two seeds, so the question was not answered; with two runs against two the smallest possible exact p is 0.33. A 30-minute window holds two to three runs under this load, not six to eight; the next batch needs 60 minutes or a quiet server, and a later start cut-off.

Independent checks: docking, cavity and force-field relaxation

In brief

Six further cycles checked the Boltz-2 poses with methods that do not share its weaknesses. Docking failed its own control. Relaxation and cavity checks agreed with Boltz-2 on one point: only the four-residue plug is clearly outside the noise.

Cycle 0

Takeaway: A second environment was needed (CUDA 12.8).

  • Design. Build one environment for docking, cavity and force-field work under the server’s data policy.
  • Build and test. Environment built. Two failures: the GPU runtime did not match the driver, and a package install upgraded numpy and left unrelated sub-packages with unmet requirements.
  • Learn. Computation moved to a second, separate environment pinned to CUDA 12.8.

Cycle 1

Takeaway: The copies are not independent; the noise floor comes from method sensitivity.

  • Design. Measure the six ketoacyl synthase copies in the 1.9 Å structure 8PRW, to get a noise floor for pocket geometry.
  • Build and test. The six copies are essentially identical (C1305 to M1251 8.5 to 8.8 Å; the M1251 chi1 angle −68° in all six). A cavity of about 175 to 215 Å³ sits at C1305 on one copy (three grid and probe settings; its volume moves by about ±20 % with those settings, and M1251 lined it in some settings only).
  • Learn. The copies are not an independent estimate of variability, so the noise floor comes from method sensitivity. The detected cavity is far smaller than a C14 chain.

Cycle 2

Takeaway: Docking failed its control and was dropped.

  • Design. Can Vina docking recover the known decanoate pose in E. coli 1F91 (positive control)?
  • Build and test. Failed. Top-ranked poses were 4.8 to 10.1 Å from the crystal pose over 18 runs (rule: 3 Å). Scores differed by about 0.3 kcal/mol across the top five poses.
  • Learn. Docking is not used to compare poses or rank ligands. Poses are judged by force-field relaxation instead.

Cycle 3

Takeaway: Tooling was fixed and the retention rule set beforehand.

  • Design. Relax Boltz-2 poses by minimisation and short restrained simulation.
  • Build and test. Slow on the CPU, then blocked by the GPU runtime and ligand-parameter errors (see the register). A one-model test then took about 40 s.
  • Learn. Tooling was fixed; the pose-retention rule was set before any pose was relaxed.

Cycle 4

Takeaway: Only the four-residue plug lies outside the noise.

  • Design. Relax all 50 Boltz-2 models (wild type with seeds 42 and 43, plug, V1254W, V1254A, M1251W, C22, C8, caffeine, glucose).
  • Build and test. 50 of 50 models stayed in the pocket (ligand RMSD median 0.6 to 1.0 Å). Interaction energy (kJ/mol, median of five): wild type −115 and −108 for the two seeds, V1254W −129, V1254A −126, M1251W −125, plug −150, C22 −142, C8 −71, caffeine −90, glucose −59.
  • Learn. The retention check could not fail, so it shows only that poses are not collapsing artefacts. Only the plug lies clearly outside the wild-type spread; single substitutions differ by about the size of the seed difference.

Outcome and limits

In brief

The structural cycles narrowed the design without deciding V1254W: Boltz-2 poses were within seed noise except for the four-residue plug, and docking did not pass its control. The experimental test of V1254W is the wet-lab construct.

What the cycles established

  • The reported overlap with M1251 was not reproduced on the laboratory GPUs, so it is not used as evidence.
  • Seed variation (ligand RMSD median 2.14 Å) was as large as the effect of single substitutions, so no conclusion about V1254W, V1254A or M1251W is drawn.
  • The four-tryptophan plug is the only variant clearly outside the wild-type spread, in interaction energy (−150 against −115 and −108 kJ/mol) and in cavity volume (74 and 107 against 139 and 120 Å3).
  • The decoys bound more weakly than C14 in the relaxed models (caffeine −90, glucose −59 kJ/mol), which is the expected direction but rests on one seed each.
  • Docking failed its positive control and was not used to rank anything.

Scope of the conclusions

  • These results are model outputs. Whether the engineered V1254W strain shifts the fatty-acid profile in baker’s yeast is measured by the wet lab (cycle E).
  • The 200 ps relaxations had a restrained backbone and all 50 poses passed, so they cannot show that a pose is stable. Unrestrained multi-nanosecond simulation is a natural extension.
  • Wild type has two seeds, V1254W and the plug two (round 6, seed 43, partly complete) and every other variant one, so there is no variance estimate. Five seeds per variant would give a variance estimate.
  • No second co-folding model was run. The alignment-based Boltz-2 positive control was not used: it needed the public E. coli sequence to be sent to an external alignment server, which was not approved. Both positive controls that were run failed: Vina docking, and (on 17 October 2026) a single-sequence Boltz-2 run on 1F91, whose best model was 4.8 Å from the crystal decanoate (median about 10 Å; unconstrained 8–12 Å; confidence 0.33–0.37). The Boltz-2 result is a weaker test than the main pipeline, so the alignment-based control would show whether the aligned pipeline recovers a known pose.
  • A tunnel-blocking effect of V1254W cannot be tested on the apo structure, because its cavity never reaches V1254.
  • No strain proposals were issued, because the pre-flight checks showed the scoring function could not support them (see Trust).

Lessons learned

In brief

Run the control before trusting the method, estimate noise before reading effects, and test a check on a case that can fail. These changed what the later cycles did.

  • Run the positive control first. Docking was tried on a known complex before it was used on ours. It failed, so it was dropped before it could rank anything. The same lesson was not applied to Boltz-2: it was used on our target for five rounds before any control was attempted, and the only control since (single-sequence, 1F91) did not recover the known pose.
  • A repeat is not a replicate. The same-seed repeat was identical and said nothing about variation. A second seed showed that noise was as large as the substitution effects, and this changed how every earlier distance was read.
  • Confidence is not evidence. Boltz-2 confidence stayed at 0.90 to 0.94 in every setup, so it could not separate them. Controls and independent methods were needed.
  • A check must be able to fail. The retention rule was set before the relaxations were run, but all 50 poses passed. A threshold set in advance is not enough if the test cannot reject; the next version needs unrestrained simulation.
  • Test the analysis on a known case. Bond-length jumps of 2.5 and 5.2 Å exposed that the docking output reorders atoms, and a missing RMSD exposed a mis-read atom type. Both result sets were renamed, not deleted, and not used.
  • Do not rely on one sample per run. The first runs used one sample each and reported an overlap that later runs, with five samples, did not reproduce.
  • Test the tooling on one case before a batch. A one-model run (about 40 s) found the GPU-runtime and ligand-parameter errors before 50 models were queued. A separate environment pinned to the driver’s CUDA version avoided a second round of conflicts.
  • Record failures with causes. Causes marked ‘possibly’ in the register are probable explanations and say so.

What we would do differently. Run a second seed in the first Boltz-2 round, and set up the pipeline positive control and unrestrained simulation at the start rather than after the fifth round. The scripts, inputs and result tables behind these cycles are held in the team’s project records.

Further cycles

In brief

Four extensions follow from what was learned: unrestrained simulation, more seeds, a second co-folding model and a positive control.

  • Unrestrained simulation. Replicated runs of 5 to 10 ns, starting from several Boltz-2 poses of wild type and V1254W, to test whether the ligand stays and whether V1254W changes the V1254-to-ligand distance beyond replicate spread.
  • More seeds. At least five seeds per variant, compared as distributions over all models rather than medians.
  • A second co-folding model as an independent check on Boltz-2, subject to licence and memory.
  • Positive control for the pipeline. A structure with a known bound fatty acid (1F91), with a locally generated alignment, reporting the ligand RMSD to the crystal pose whatever the outcome.
